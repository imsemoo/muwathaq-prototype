## Users

- Human-rights organisations, lawyers, researchers, investigative journalists, academic and rights institutions (client brief). They come to find a documented file, an incident record, an international report or a legal article, check where a claim comes from, and cite it.
- Contributors: witnesses or members of the public who submit an incident or a piece of evidence for review.
- Supporters who donate to keep the documentation independent.
- People or bodies named in a file who request a correction or a right of reply.

## Product Purpose

موثّق (MUWATHAQ) is a digital platform for documenting, verifying, preserving and legally analysing evidence about crimes committed by perpetrators of the Israeli occupation, and linking each incident to the international legal texts it may engage. The problem it answers (brief): digital evidence is scattered, exposed to deletion or tampering, and hard to verify for source and context. Success, per the brief's indicators: one live platform, 500 documented incidents, 300 reviewed files, at least 6 reports, use by at least 5 beneficiary institutions, and complete verification records on 90% of published files (year one targets, not results). Strategic link: SDG 16 (justice, accountability, rule of law).

## Positioning

One platform that joins digital documentation, evidence verification, geographic analysis and the linking of facts to international law (brief: «القيمة المضافة»). Its discipline is the separation every file shows: accusation is not conviction; evidence, claim and conclusion are kept apart; verifying a piece of material is not the same as establishing anyone's criminal responsibility.

## Operating Context

- Person files: each carries a legal status with the issuing body and date. Taxonomy from the client mockup: convicted by a court judgment, wanted under an arrest warrant, formally charged, named in a documented investigation or report, subject of allegations under verification. A warrant or a report is never presented as a conviction.
- Incident records: date or period, location marked precise or approximate (approximate to protect people), summary, type, linked persons, evidence, and a legal analysis per act: the rule, why it may apply, and what still has to be proven.
- Evidence records: type (video, image, satellite imagery, document, rights report, testimony published with consent, official statement), context, known limits, chain of custody and revision history.
- Verification levels: not started, under verification, partially verified, specific elements verified, complete per the platform's methodology (never a finding of criminal responsibility), needs re-review.
- Reports library: each document labelled by its legal nature (court decision, ICC document, ICJ order, UN report, commission of inquiry, rights organisation, investigative journalism, legal analysis, expert report).
- Legal rules library: international humanitarian law, international criminal law, international human-rights law, rules of individual and command responsibility; short paraphrases that always point to the official text.
- Methodology: ten steps (collect, register source and custody, authenticity and context, independent comparison, geo- and chrono-location, documenting limits, legal review, data protection, publication decision, continuous correction).
- Contribution: nothing is published automatically; an eight-step review pipeline and its states (received, initial screening, digital verification, legal review, needs more information, approved, rejected, archived unpublished).
- Corrections and right of reply, published policies, revision logs.

## Capabilities and Constraints

## Product Principles

1. Status before name: a person's legal standing, its issuing body and date are read before anything else, and a warrant never looks like a conviction.
2. Show the seams: what is verified, what is still pending and the known limits stay visible on every record.
3. Protect people: victims and witnesses are shielded, locations can be approximate, graphic material is never shown without consent and warning.
4. Correct in public: every change is logged with its date; corrections are visible, not silent.
5. Everything links: incident, evidence, law, person and report point to each other and to the original source.
